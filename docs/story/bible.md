# With Great Power: the story bible

This is the bible for Season 1, “The Downloads”, written for writers and game designers. It covers the city, the cast, the five case files, The Daily Rug, and how each mode tells story.

- **When this file and a case doc disagree:** this file wins on story. The case’s contract (`docs/chapters/case-00N.md`) wins on mechanics.
- **Status:** 001 is live; 002 and 003 are being built; 004 and 005 are outlined.

---

## 1. Premise and tone

### The city

The city is a comic-book parody metropolis whose economy runs on “mainnet”. Everything is a transaction: buying a loaf, crossing a bridge, voting on a toll. “Night falls on the city. The villains are already out.”

| District | Canon line | In story | Landmarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sector 7 | Gas stations, glider pads, very high fees. | Gas Goblin’s turf: fumes, and price boards that only go up | The glider pads; the all-night print shop at the gas station; the “GAS 400% OFF\* \*on” billboard |
| Mempool Market | Everyone queues. Somebody always cuts in. | Trade and queues, and Sandwich Man’s hunting ground. Mary Jpeg works the rooftops for the light. | The queue that never ends; Gwei Stacy’s stall; Aunt Mainnet’s Loaf, her weekend stall where the Market meets Queens Block |
| Bridge District | Eight bridges, one very bad one. | Crossings, tolls and Doc Octochain. The Daily Rug’s offices are here. | The eight bridges; the Rug building; *the* lamppost |
| Queens Block | Aunt Mainnet’s street. Never goes down. | Home: Aunt Mainnet and Uncle Gen | The stoop; four flights of stairs; the kitchen node where the bite happened |
| Validator Heights | Towers full of nodes, and copies of copies. | Votes, identity and the Sybil | The Stacks (the old public archive, now empty); the clerk’s office; Gwei’s water tower |

### The joke engine

The game plays crypto and internet culture as comic-book melodrama. Take a dry idea, make it literal and physical, and give it the stakes of a 1960s splash page:

- A Sybil attack is a man who is thousands of himself.
- A bridge exploit is a man with eight arms who won’t let go of a bridge.
- Front-running is a man made of bread who cuts in line, front and back.
- Right-click-save is a hooded thief who freezes people into flat, compressed copies.

There are two registers:

- **Loud:** Bearson’s caps headlines, villain taunts, and comic sound effects (“CLICK!”, “+CLUE!”, “OOF!”).
- **Quiet:** small, sincere moments. Aunt Mainnet’s slice for the road. Gen’s speech landing anyway. Mary’s first “gm”.

Every case has exactly one quiet beat, and nothing undercuts it in the same panel. The joke waits for the next one.

### Rules of the voice

- **The player is never the butt.** Bearson calls the hero a menace, but the paper never mocks a player’s skill, wallet, body or real identity. A loss is slapstick (“flattened”), never humiliation.
- **Villains are ridiculous, not cruel.** Their schemes are petty and specific (a toll, a loaf, a vote), and each villain has a soft spot.
- **No real people, brands, projects, tokens, exchanges or influencers.** Generic nouns are fine: mainnet, gas, gwei, nonce, mempool, validator, fork, bridge, JPEG, GIF, CSV.
- **Rewards are XP, Bits (from verified runs) and soulbound badges, and nothing else.** In-world “fees”, “tolls” and “gwei” are jokes, not payments. No copy promises money or prizes.
- **Sincerity is allowed.** This is a parody of a story about responsibility, and it means the responsibility part.

## 2. The season arc: “The Downloads”

### The backstory, in order

1. **Genesis.** The city’s first block. Uncle Gen was there; Aunt Mainnet says he was just early.
2. **The Stacks.** The city’s public archive was a tower in Validator Heights that kept every edition, receipt, post and name.
   - **The Archive Node:** the small eight-legged validator that ran it.
   - **Archie Vist:** the night archivist, who shushed people.
3. **The Great Rug.** One night, the Stacks went down. Nobody agrees which night, because the record of it went too.
   - **What happened:** nobody hacked it and nobody stole it. Somebody pulled it.
   - **What was lost:** everything from before, in one block.
   - **Archie:** he was on shift and had saved nothing, because he believed it was permanent.
   - **The Archive Node:** it crawled out of the wreck looking for the most reliable machine in the city. It found Aunt Mainnet’s kitchen node.
4. **The morning after.** Bearson’s old paper went down with the Stacks, so he printed on actual paper (“Paper doesn’t go down”). He called it *The Daily Rug*, as a dare. That edition is Page 0.
5. **The Right-Clicker.** Archie came out holding only his cursor, and he has right-clicked everything since. He traced the Archive Node’s faint signal to Queens Block. He was outside the kitchen window on the night of the bite, and he filmed it.
6. **The bite.** The Node was rebooting Aunt Mainnet’s node. (She never goes down. Her router is another matter.) The Archive Node bit him, and he hasn’t missed a block since.
7. **Case 001.** The Right-Clicker saves Mary Jpeg, and the Node webs him on Doc’s bridge. The folder bursts (“His Downloads folder is emptied”). That burst is **the leak**.

### Why he saves people

He thinks he’s rescuing them. Everything in this city compresses, gets reposted, or gets rugged.

- **Mary:** he saved her because each repost made her blurrier, and he wanted to keep her “before there was nothing left”.
- **What he can’t see:** a saved person is a frozen person.
- **The wound:** “Saves everything, owns nothing.” Owning means you can lose it. A copy can’t be taken from you, and it can’t hug you back either.

### What the Downloads folder is

The folder isn’t a hoard. It’s the Stacks, rebuilt one right-click at a time.

- **What’s in it:** people, receipts, recipes, the voter roll, blueprints.
- **The Sybil:** ten thousand screenshots of him. Archie tried to save the real one once, got 10,000 copies, and none of them was him.
- **The flaw:** every file is an unsigned copy. A copy can’t be verified, so it can’t be restored. Only the Archive Node’s signature makes a copy real again.

### The leak

The folder burst on Doc Octochain’s deck, so Doc got first pick. He kept the eighth bridge’s blueprint and became the fence for the rest, selling off his bridge and shipping files cross-chain.

- **The Sybil** bought the voter roll and the screenshots of himself.
- **Gas Goblin** bought Aunt Mainnet’s recipes.

Each case file closes one scheme and recovers one key file.

**The lamppost:** the Right-Clicker webbed to it at the end of 001 was a CTRL+C copy (see Corrections). The real one watches all season from far-off rooftops. He’s somewhere in every case’s ending panel, screenshotting it.

### The files

| Case | File | What it is | Who had it | What they did with it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | `mary_jpeg (1).jpg` | Mary Jpeg, about 1 MB | The Right-Clicker | Kept her “safe”, frozen on a bridge deck |
| 002 | `voter_roll_FINAL.csv` | The city’s voter roll: 10,001 rows, 10,000 of them the same man | The Sybil | Registered 10,000 screenshots of himself as voters |
| 003 | `recipes_DO_NOT_SHARE (1).pdf` | Aunt Mainnet’s recipe book; page 41 is the Never-Goes-Down Loaf | Gas Goblin | Decided “never goes down” meant fees, and struck |
| 004 | `bridge_8_blueprint (2).png` | The eighth bridge’s blueprint, which is a screenshot | Doc Octochain | Built a bridge from a picture of a bridge |
| 005 | `archive_backup_DO_NOT_DELETE.zip`, `bite_cam_04.mp4`, `me.txt` (0 KB) | The whole Stacks, the night of the bite, and him | The Right-Clicker | Waited all season for the one validator who can sign it |

### The turn

The folder holds the Stacks’ backup, and its last file is the bite. These are the same secret: the radioactive validator that bit the Node was the Archive Node.

- **What the Node carries:** the city’s archive key. It’s the only signature that can turn the Downloads (copies) back into the city (owned).
- **The confession:** the Right-Clicker has screenshotted the Node all season because he needs him. “I’ve been saving copies of you for months. I needed you to be real.”
- **`me.txt`:** it’s 0 KB. In a folder that holds everything, the one thing he never saved was himself. His name was lost in the Great Rug, and the restore gives it back.

**The theme in one line:** a copy is free; making something real costs gas. “With great power comes great gas fees”: whoever has the power pays to make things real for everyone else. The part nobody stays for is that you don’t have to pay alone.

## 3. The cast

Everyone keeps their canon line, and their in-game taunt and defeat (`rules/data.ts`, `rules/chapters.ts`), as written.

### The Node (the player’s hero)

- **Canon:** bitten by a radioactive validator while rebooting Aunt Mainnet’s node; “hasn’t missed a block since”. Players name their hero, so copy uses the hero’s name. The Rug says “he” out of habit, as today’s editions do; anywhere else, prefer the name.
- **Wants:** to help the neighbours, and to keep the streak.
- **Running gag:** his uptime, which the Rug counts sourly. Selfie requests at the worst moment.
- **Secret:** he carries the Archive Node, the city’s archive key.
- **Turn (005):** decides who pays the gas.
- **Voice:** mostly silent; he speaks through the player’s choices. The interview errand’s humble, brag and cheeky lines are the template.

### Aunt Mainnet

- **Canon:** never goes down; bakes on weekends.
- **Wants:** everyone fed, and everyone up.
- **Running gags:**
  - “She says she’s fine. She is not fine.”
  - A slice for the road, for everyone (villains included).
  - Her uptime counter in the Rug.
- **Secret:** she knows who the Node is, and always has. She knew something lived in her router, and left it crumbs.
- **Turn:** in 003 she won’t come down the ladder. In 005 she either catches the Node when he goes down or signs first.
- **Voice:** “There you are, sweetie! Still up? Me too. I never go down.”

### Uncle Gen

- **Canon:** gives “with great power comes great gas fees” speeches.
- **Wants:** to finish the speech once.
- **Running gags:**
  - Nobody stays for the end (“He shouts the end of it after you. Twice.”).
  - He mixes up “great responsibility” and “great gas fees”, and insists they’re the same thing. They are.
  - His Rug column always breaks off at “continued on page 9”. There is no page 9.
- **Secret:**
  - **Genesis:** he cast the city’s first ballot, his “genesis ballot”.
  - **Archie:** he knew him at the Stacks (“You used to shush me”).
  - **The speech:** he knows how it ends.
- **Turn:** in 002 his one vote sinks the toll; in 005 he finishes the speech.
- **Voice:** “Remember, kid. With great power comes great gas fees. I’ll say it again at dinner.”

### Mary Jpeg

- **Canon:** extremely online, slightly compressed; answers to “gm”.
- **Wants:** the shot, and the timeline.
- **Running gag:** her quality is printed after her name, and it drops a point every time the Rug reposts her (94%, 93%…). Captions like “rude”.
- **Secret:** she has never seen her original, because she’s a repost of a repost. The Right-Clicker was her first follower, back when he liked every post.
- **Turn (005):** offered her RAW original, she keeps herself compressed: “This one’s me.” She’s the first to forgive him.
- **Voice:** “One photo! For the timeline!”

### Gwei Stacy

- **Canon:** swings in from another chain; counts everything in gwei; runs the Mempool Market stall; stands on a water tower in Validator Heights at her errand.
- **Wants:** a fair price, a clean invoice, and backup on her own terms.
- **Running gag:** invoices for everything, to absurd precision. “Your funeral, web-head.”
- **Secret:** her home chain went dark in the same Great Rug, and gwei is the last unit she has from home. She watches the eighth bridge from her water tower because it’s the only way back.
- **Turn (004):** she cuts the bridge’s far end so the Downloads can’t leave, closing her own way home. “Put it on my tab.”
- **Voice:** “Need backup, web-head? I’ll invoice you in gwei.”

### J. Jonah Bearson

- **Canon:** editor of The Daily Rug. He thinks the hero is a menace, prints every night and shorts everything.
- **Wants:** pictures, the scoop, and to be right.
- **Running gags:**
  - “Get me pictures!”
  - Corrections that double down.
  - Bylines like “who already knows the answer”.
  - “Bears were right.”
- **Secret:** his old paper went down in the Great Rug. Page 0 is his, and he has kept every edition since: “Bearson never forgets.”
- **Turn (005):** he hires the Right-Clicker as staff photographer and keeper of back issues, the one man in the city who takes nothing but pictures. He prints one thank-you, in very small type.
- **Voice:** “Menace! That web-headed menace is at it again! Get me pictures!”

### Gas Goblin

- **Canon:** fee spikes, fee bombs, a glider; based in Sector 7.
- **Wants:** fees that only ever go up.
- **Running gag:** the percentage in his taunt only rises: four hundred, then five hundred, then “a number I made up”. “Surge pricing!”
- **Secret:**
  - **The fees:** he has never paid one in his life. His glider runs on everyone else’s, so every fee he spikes fills his tank.
  - **The loaf:** he’s also Aunt Mainnet’s best customer. Every Saturday, a tall man in a trench coat and an unconvincing mustache is first in line for two loaves.
- **Turn (003):** his own strike shuts the bakery. No Saturday loaf.
- **Voice:** “Fees are up four hundred percent, web-head!” / “No! My fees! My beautiful fees!”

### Sandwich Man

- **Canon:** front-runs you; sets bread traps; works the Mempool Market.
- **Wants:** to be first, and last. Whoever ends up in the middle is the filling.
- **Running gag:** he’s already in every queue: the Rug’s classifieds, the case files, right beside the Top swingers column (never on it).
- **Secret:** there’s one line he has never been first in: Aunt Mainnet’s on a Saturday. A tall man in a trench coat always beats him to it, and it eats at him.
- **Turn (005):** he holds the door. “After you.” It’s the first time he has let anyone go first.
- **Voice:** “I was here before you. I’ll be here after you.” / “Hey! I was in line first!”

### Doc Octochain

- **Canon:** holds the bridge; chain arms; cross-chain everything.
- **Wants:** every chain there is, plugged into his arms, each with a toll.
- **Running gag:** his arms end up knotted together (“Again!”). “8 arms, 0 refunds.”
- **Secret:** he isn’t holding the bridge; the bridge is holding him. The eighth bridge was built from a screenshot, his arms are bolted into its cables, and they’re all that keeps it up. He rigged the toll and fenced the leak to build a real bridge someday. Maybe.
- **Turn (004):** when the Node takes the weight on webs, he lets go, and the bridge holds.
- **Voice:** “Eight arms. Eight chains. And every one of them is mine.”

### The Sybil

- **Canon:** thousands of copies of himself; lives in Validator Heights.
- **Wants:** a majority, of everything, unanimously.
- **Running gag:** he always says “we”, and demands a recount whenever he loses.
- **Secret:** he isn’t sure which one is him either. He keeps rules for it (shadow, blink, ballot hand) and checks them in the mirror.
- **Turn:** in 002, marked and counted as exactly one, he finds he likes it. In 005 he signs once. He checked.
- **Voice:** “We vote, unanimously, that you lose.” / “We… demand a recount.”

### The Right-Clicker (Archie Vist)

- **Canon:** saves everything, owns nothing; screenshots people into his “Downloads”. Hooded, with a giant cursor.
- **Wants:** nothing ever lost again.
- **Running gags:**
  - He screenshots everything, including the lamppost he’s webbed to.
  - He leaves copies of himself behind.
  - He turns up in the distance of every ending panel.
  - “Nice suit. Mind if I save it?”
- **Secret:** the folder is the Stacks, he filmed the bite, and `me.txt` is 0 KB.
- **Turn (005):** he signs something instead of copying it. He gets a job at the Rug, and a photo credit on the first picture he gives away.
- **Voice:** “Hold still. I’m saving you.”

## 4. The case files

Every case follows the same shape:

1. **Intro comic:** four panels: the Rug’s front page, the evidence, Bearson’s line, and the case file stamp.
2. **Investigation:** three clues, in any order, each with a case note.
3. **Set piece:** multi-phase from 002 on.
4. **Rescue.**
5. **The Rug’s front page:** an Extra edition.

Each case recovers one Downloads file, under its own ruleset tag (`c00N-1`). The first verified clear mints a soulbound `trophy:badge:case-00N` and pays `CASE_XP` (100).

### 001 “Missing: Mary Jpeg” (live)

Mary Jpeg went quiet after a night in the Bridge District. Somebody right-clicked.

- **Intro:**
  1. The Rug: “JPEG GONE”.
  2. The MISSING poster: “About 1 MB; answers to ‘gm’”.
  3. Bearson: “Find her before the other papers do. And get me pictures. Uncompressed ones.”
  4. The case file, stamped 001.
- **Clues:** on Bridge District roofs.
  - Torn tabs: “Somebody tore off a tab. Then all of them.”
  - A cursor scorch on a billboard: “Left-click? No. Right.”
  - Loose pixels trailing to the bridge.
- **Set piece:** the Right-Clicker on Doc’s bridge deck. He takes 8 hits.
  - **SCREENSHOT:** an aim cone. Sidestep it, or you’re SAVED (frozen); he’s open while he reloads.
  - **CTRL+C:** two paper copies block a hit each.
  - **DRAG:** a lasso. Break it with a blow or a zip, and he staggers.
- **Rescue:** hold web beside frozen, pixelated Mary for about 3 s while two paper copies slap at you. She says “gm”.
- **The Rug:** “JPEG FOUND. SLIGHTLY MORE COMPRESSED.” She’s back at roughly 94% quality. Caption: “Pictured: Mary Jpeg, restored. Mostly.”
- **Revealed later:** the folder burst on that deck (the leak), the lamppost held a copy, and he saved her to stop her compressing.

### 002 “The Sybil Vote” (built: `docs/chapters/case-002.md`)

**Logline:** the city’s block vote on a new bridge toll is rigged. The Sybil floods it with copies of himself, pirated from the Right-Clicker’s leaked Downloads folder: thousands of screenshots of the Sybil, printed as voters. Find the real one, mark him, and get the last honest ballot into the box.

**Setting:** Validator Heights rooftops. The polling station is on the roof of the Stacks, so put the plaque by the stairs: “Public Archive. Night shift: A. V.”

**Intro comic (as shipped, `/play/cases/002`):**

1. The Rug: “TURNOUT: 10,000%”. “Polls in Validator Heights report record numbers. Officials report the numbers all have the same face.”
2. The line at the polling station, five of him, one in colour: “We’re all here to vote.” / “Some of us more than others.”
3. Bearson: “Find the real one. Then find me a headline.” His aside: “And make sure your uncle votes. He keeps calling.”
4. The case file, stamped 002: the facts (turnout 10,000% and rising; voters: one man, saved many times; wanted: the original), the suspect, and Uncle Gen: “With great power comes great gas fees. And one vote. Mine.”

**Investigation:** three ballot drop boxes on Validator Heights roofs. Each has a clue wedged in it that reveals one tell of the real Sybil, set by the night’s seed. In-world, the copies were printed in batches, and each batch got one detail wrong.

| Clue | What it is | Tell | Case note as shipped (he has it / he doesn’t) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booth footage | A CCTV still, taped to a drop box by a billboard | Whether the real one casts a shadow | “In the footage he casts a shadow. Most of his copies are flat files: they don’t.” / “In the footage he casts no shadow: he stood in the booth’s shade. Some copies have shadows baked in.” |
| Looping sticker | An “I voted” GIF. Some copies were saved as GIFs and blink on a loop. | Whether the real one blinks | “The GIF copies blink on a loop, and so does he: the real one blinks.” / “The real one hasn’t blinked since the polls opened. Only the GIF copies blink.” |
| Smudged signature | 10,000 ballots, one signature | Which hand he holds his ballot in | “Every signature slants left. The real one holds his ballot in his left hand.” / “…slants right. … in his right hand.” |

The eight voters are one of each mix of the three tells, so all three clues single him out; two leave a coin toss; one leaves four suspects. In the city, a shadow is a shadow, a blink is an eye badge over his head that shuts now and then, and the ballot is a card held out to one side.

**Set piece:** the rooftop polling station.

- **The lineup:** eight identical voters stand in two rows of booths. You mark the one who matches all three tells by hitting him.
- **A wrong mark:** it pops a copy and forks an extra copy into the fight. (“We object.”)

The fight has three phases:

- **“Polls open”:**
  - **Fake votes:** slow goo ballots that you sidestep.
  - **FORK VOTE:** the copies call one. A line splits the rooftop, and the side you stand on is orphaned.
  - **Two ways out:** either cross to the canonical side before it closes, or pop enough voting copies to deny quorum. Denying quorum leaves the Sybil stunned and open.
- **“Recount”:** at half health he hides in a ring of copies again, so the tells matter mid-fight.
- **“Runoff”:** faster fork votes.

**Rescue:** the last copy runs off with Uncle Gen’s original ballot, his “genesis ballot”. Catch it and drop it in the ballot box beside Gen. The toll fails by one vote: his.

**Quiet beat:** Gen watches it go in. “Every block since the first one. Never missed.” The Node, who hasn’t missed one since the bite, says nothing.

**The Rug:**

- **Headline:** TOLL FAILS BY ONE VOTE
- **Dek:** “The bridge toll is voted down, 1 to 0. Nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine other ballots were the same man and didn’t count.”
- **Story (as shipped):**
  - “The Sybil stuffed the Validator Heights ballot box with copies of himself, saved from a Downloads folder that should have stayed shut. [The hero] picked the real one out of the line and webbed him to the box.”
  - “The deciding vote was cast by Uncle Gen, who has voted in every election since genesis and would like that noted. This paper notes it. The receipts for ten thousand voters, meanwhile, all point at the bridge.”
- **Caption:** “Pictured: Uncle Gen, counted. The Sybil, not.”
- **Byline:** “By J. Jonah Bearson, who counted himself”.
- **Next edition’s Correction:** “Yesterday’s paper demanded a recount. So did the Sybil. We regret agreeing with him.”

**What’s recovered, and what it plants:**

- **File:** `voter_roll_FINAL.csv`, which goes back to the clerk.
- **Thread:** every copy carries a receipt reading “Paid by Doc’s Bridge Co. 8 arms, 0 refunds.” Ten thousand receipts point at the bridge.
- **Badge:** the soulbound `trophy:badge:case-002` on the first verified clear (its sticker: “One vote”).

### 003 “Fee Strike” (a brief for the agent building it)

This is a complete suggestion. Adapt the mechanics to the sim, but keep the beats, the file, the ending page and the thread.

**Logline:** Gas Goblin spikes every fee in the Mempool Market until Aunt Mainnet’s bakery can’t pay for its own transactions. Chase him across the Market rooftops before Saturday dawns on a dark oven.

**Stakes:**

- If the bakery’s transactions don’t clear by dawn, Aunt Mainnet misses a weekend. It would be the first “down” of her life.
- Meanwhile every Market queue stalls, and Sandwich Man feasts on the pending line.

**File:** `recipes_DO_NOT_SHARE (1).pdf`, Aunt Mainnet’s recipe book.

- **The Goblin’s reading:** he bought it from the leak and read page 41, *The Never-Goes-Down Loaf*. He concluded it’s the secret to fees that never go down.
- **The plan:** strike until she sells, then own “never goes down” forever.
- **The catch:** it’s bread. The last line on the page reads “Secret ingredient: bake it for someone.” He doesn’t get it.

**Intro comic (suggested):**

1. The Rug: “FEES UP 400%. LOAF DOWN?”
2. Her dark OPEN sign, and a queue in the dawn cold.
3. Bearson: “Find out who’s pricing this city out. I already know. Prove it.” His aside: “And bring me a loaf.”
4. The case file, stamped 003.

**Investigation:** like 002’s tells, each clue can set one chase variable from the seed, so a player who reads them can cut him off.

| Clue | Where | Case note | Can set |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stuck receipt | Taped to her stall | “Status: pending. Fee: 400,000,000,000 gwei. Gwei Stacy circled it twice.” | Where tonight’s fee board starts |
| Exhaust trail | Green soot on rooftop vents | “Glider exhaust. Smells like four hundred percent.” | Which of three rooftop routes he flies |
| The mustache | Snagged on the bakery awning | “A fake mustache. Crumbs in it. Somebody here is a regular.” | Which gas-station roof he refuels on |

**The chase.** His moves are jokes that become mechanics. Keep it chase-centric, so it teaches run, sprint and climb as well as swing.

| Joke | Suggested mechanic |
|---|---|
| “Surge pricing!” | When he cackles, a price board flips to +400%. For a few seconds, web anchors in that block cost triple fluid, so cross those roofs on foot. |
| Fee bombs | Dropped behind him, they burst into green fee clouds that slow you and drain fluid, each popping a “+400%” tag. |
| Glider dive | A telegraphed dive. Dodge it, and he clips the “GAS 400% OFF\*” billboard, which opens him up. |
| Priority fee | He pays himself to the front of the line: a dash along the Market’s sky queue of pending parcels. Boost rings (“gas refunds”) keep you in touch. |
| The base fee rises | A soft timer: the Market’s fee board climbs while the chase runs. At “max”, the bakery’s lights flicker and his bombs grow. |
| Out of gas | His tank fills on everyone else’s fees. Every popped fee bomb and every refund ring lowers the board and drains it. When it’s empty, he glides down onto the bakery awning (gliding is the one free thing in Sector 7). Finish with a short grounded fight or a catch-and-tie. |

**Sandwich Man (cameo):**

- **On the roofs:** he front-runs your landing. He’s on the roof a moment before you (“I was here before you”), with a bread trap.

## 6. How each mode tells story

### Patrol nights (ranked: “the beat”)

A patrol night is the Node’s beat: four stops, swinging between them, with villain fights and ally errands, and the boss last.

- **The boss:** if the last edition set a quest (“a villain who got away tonight”), that villain is the boss.
- **Errands:** recurring sitcom scenes, each one a choice that moves Responsibility and Reputation:
  - Aunt Mainnet’s groceries;
  - Gen’s thought;
  - Mary’s selfie;
  - Bearson’s interview;
  - Gwei on her water tower.
- **The Rug prints every night from what happened:**
  - **The lead:** from the boss result.
  - **“Also in this edition”:** from the errands, plus reckless-stunt stories.
  - **The interview quote:** it runs as “Masked menace speaks” (“…to this newspaper, under duress”).
  - **The reader poll, and tomorrow’s quest.**

**The arc on patrol nights is page-side only.** The side columns read the save’s case progress (the Downloads box, Corrections about the last case, a Classifieds teaser for the next) and decorate the page around the edition.

**Season 1 rule:** under ruleset 4, the patrol’s words and the edition generator are frozen, because verified replays write them into saves. That covers `data.ts` taunts, defeats and errand text, and `edition()` in `engine.ts`.

### Case files (story nights, off the boards)

The shape is in section 4: intro comic, investigation, set piece, rescue, and the Rug’s front page, which leads the next edition as an Extra. Each case recovers one Downloads file and plants the next thread.

- **Off the boards:** cases are never ranked and never mint Bits.
- **The first verified clear:** closes the case, pays 100 XP, and mints the soulbound “case closed” badge.
- **Later clears:** they only improve the best time.
- **Guests:** they clear locally (“Sign in to file the case”).
- **Locked cases:** they wait as “Bearson is still writing.” The night before a case opens, its teaser runs in the Classifieds.

### The free-roam city: torn Daily Rug pages

The run, sprint, climb and swing sandbox hides **31 torn pages of the Rug**: six per district, plus Page 0.

- **What a page is:** a single column clipping that adds lore.
- **Numbering:** by district: `S7-1`…`S7-6`, then `MM-`, `BD-`, `QB-`, `VH-`.
- **The verb:** each page asks for one traversal verb: a run across a roof gap, a sprint along a short line, a climb up a tower or water tower, or a swing under a bridge.

**Each district’s six pages:**

1. A backstory fragment of the Great Rug.
2. A correction.
3. A Menace Watch or blotter item.
4. An Ask Aunt Mainnet letter or a soapbox piece.
5. A hint classified, pointing to another page or a traversal secret.
6. A “Downloads” page: a LOST FILE classified that foreshadows a case.

## 7. The style sheet

**Naming:**

- **Allies:** a family word plus infrastructure (Aunt Mainnet, Uncle Gen), or a classic comic first name plus a web noun (Mary Jpeg, Gwei Stacy).
- **Villains:** a comic archetype plus an exploit (Gas Goblin, Doc Octochain).
- **Files:** real download debris: ` (1)`, `_FINAL`, `(final) (2)`, `DO_NOT_DELETE`, `DO_NOT_SHARE`. Lowercase with underscores, and the extension is part of the joke (a bridge that’s a `.png`).

**Headlines:**

- Write them in sentence case in code; the page sets them in caps.
- Six words at most, pun first, present tense.
- A two-beat full stop is fine (“Fees fall. Loaf rises.”).
- Bearson’s spin goes in the dek.

**Deks, stories and captions:**

- **Deks:** one or two sentences, facts first and spin second, ending on the turn.
- **Stories:** two short paragraphs, using the hero’s name from the save, with Bearson getting the last word.
- **Captions:** start “Pictured:” and add one deflating aside (“Mostly.”).

**Taunts and notes:**

- **Taunts:** ten words at most: a crypto idea made physical, plus a jab at the suit or the swinging, never at the player.
- **Defeats:** one line, with a callback (“Again!”).
- **Case notes:** twelve words at most, ending on the clue’s point (“Left-click? No. Right.”).

**Typography:**

- Curly quotes and apostrophes.
- Sentence case for headings and buttons, with verbs on buttons (“Start the case”).
- Numbers as numerals, with separators (10,000).

**Banned:**

- **Real names:** real people, brands, projects, tokens, exchanges and influencers.
- **Money:** promises of money or prizes, giveaways, “airdrops”, real prices or exchange rates.
- **Scam jokes:** seed phrases, wallet drainers and other scam mechanics, which look like the real thing.
- **Punching down:** no jokes about people who lost real money, or about players’ skill, bodies or identities.
- **Real-world politics:** 002’s vote is about a bridge toll, and it stays there.
